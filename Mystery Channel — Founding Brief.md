# The Unexplained, Investigated — Founding Brief

**Working codename for a proposed Channel 3.** Prepared 2026-09-06. Draft — not committed;
the pilot decides. Companion artifact (same content, readable/shareable):

## Assessment

There is a real channel here, and the `video-os` stack is the edge — almost nobody in
this niche can build original motion graphics at the level the Remotion engine already
ships. The niche pays well (**$6–18 CPM**, $4–12 RPM; US/UK higher) and rewards exactly
what the operation is built for: slow, dense, heavily-visual episodes.

Two hard constraints:

1. **Not a 100%-code channel.** The genre's credibility runs on *real* photographs,
   footage and documents — a licensing + archival workload NIF never had.
2. **The ceiling channels (LEMMiNO, Nexpo) publish 4–6×/year and win on depth**; the
   floor (Top-5 mystery farms) had a reported ~35M channels purged in Jan 2026. The lane
   is the disciplined middle — Fascinating Horror's cadence with Visual Venture's visual
   ambition.

**Recommendation: greenlight as a pilot** — one episode, full pipeline, licensing
included — before committing the channel. The pilot is disposable; its job is to
de-risk the workflow, not to go viral.

## §1 · The opportunity

Not "a mystery channel." **Mystery + investigation + visual explanation**, where the
graphics are the investigation tool.

Positions already taken: **Visual Venture** owns "dark internet documentary,"
**Fascinating Horror** owns "calm factual disaster," a dozen narration channels own
"cold case retold." Unoccupied at scale: **a real unanswered question, reconstructed
visually, evidence shown on screen** — closer to how *Defunctland* treats theme parks
than how true-crime treats a murder.

**Timing.** Jan 2026 YouTube enforcement wave — reported ~35M channels removed under the
renamed "inauthentic content" policy. Flagged pattern: synthetic narration over
stock-footage loops, templated thumbnails, unsustainable pacing (= the Top-5 farm). That
model is now penalised. Original built visuals + real research + disclosed cloned voice +
monthly cadence runs *with* the current.

**Visual grammar to make recognisable:**
`REAL WORLD → EVIDENCE → RECONSTRUCTION → WHAT DOESN'T FIT → EXPLANATIONS → THE UNRESOLVED QUESTION`

## §4 · Strengths & risks ("trial strength")

**Strong:**

- **Economics.** True-crime / mystery narration: $6–18 CPM ($11 avg), $4–12 RPM; US/UK
  $12–22. One of YouTube's three highest-RPM long-form niches in 2026.
- **Watch time & binge.** An unresolved question is the strongest retention device on the
  platform. Evergreen back-catalogue.
- **Title/thumb surface.** "5 people vanished from the same place. Here's what we know."
- **Format tailwind.** Visual Venture +75% views/yr; multiple sub-500K channels +100%/yr.
- **Stack fit.** Slow cadence, dense visuals, disclosed synthetic voice — all rewarded.

**Dangerous:**

- **Not code-only.** Real photos/footage/docs/maps → new licensing + archival workload
  every episode. Biggest change from NIF.
- **Demonetisation edges.** Graphic imagery, crime-scene photos, bodies, shock framing →
  per-video demonetisation (Jan 2026 rules). Manageable — make it a house rule.
- **Research integrity is existential.** Hbomberguy's 2023 "Plagiarism and You(Tube)"
  showed Internet Historian's "Man in Cave" was lifted near-verbatim from a book;
  permanent damage. Every fact traces to a logged source; write from primaries in your
  own words.
- **Sensitivity.** Real victims/families. "Respectful, non-sensational" is not optional —
  it's also what keeps advertisers.
- **Top-of-funnel saturation.** Generic "unsolved mysteries" is crowded. Defence = pillar
  discipline + the built-reconstruction format nobody small is doing.
- **Slow to compound.** Depth-first channels take 8–15 episodes to find an audience.

**Net:** higher upside than an engineering channel on CTR / watch time / binge /
identity; lower on advertiser cleanliness, but the gap is closable with a disciplined
house style. Worth doing *if* the archival workload and research bar are accepted.

## §6 · Visual language — and yes, the graphics are feasible

**Is the Danny Why style feasible? Easily.** Everything in that reference is *simpler*
than what NIF ships every episode:

- Dark gradient bg — one CSS radial gradient.
- Thin glowing line-art icons/figures — SVG strokes + an SVG blur/glow filter.
- Kinetic UI mock-ups (players, "$" buttons, LIVE tags) — the `UIReveal` archetype does
  this now.
- Floating label pills ("DAY 2") — a `<Tag>` with a spring entrance.
- Comparison diagrams (`YOUTUBE ≈ TIKTOK`) — two icons + an animated operator glyph.
- **Stick figure typing at a desk** — a 5-part SVG rig (torso, head, 2 arm segments) +
  monitor + a looping 6-frame hand-tap cycle + blinking cursor + typewriter text on the
  screen. Half a day, once → reusable component.

`nif-house-style` already lists "Danny Why (line-art depth)" as a reference. The engine
is past this.

**But don't copy that look.** The Danny Why aesthetic is the generic modern-explainer /
SaaS look — clean, glowing, corporate. On a *mystery* channel it reads as "onboarding
video," not "investigation." It's also the same palette family NIF uses (cream / serif /
warm accent) — it would blur the two channels.

**The look this channel wants** — the investigator's desk, not the explainer's
whiteboard:

- **Evidence board** — pinned photos, index cards, string, handwritten annotation.
- **Documents** — real scans, highlight sweeps, redaction bars, margin notes, stamps.
- **Maps** — real geography (MapLibre, Runbook §20), route lines drawn on, last-known
  pins, search grids.
- **Timeline** — spine of most episodes; events drop in, contradictions flare.
- **Archival media** — real photos/footage, consistent grade (slight desaturation, grain,
  frame/vignette), timestamp burn-in for "footage."
- **Type** — a documentary serif/slab for titles, a monospace for evidence tags &
  timestamps, a clean sans for analysis. One restrained accent = "this is the anomaly."
- **Never** — gore, crime-scene photos, jump-scares, "you won't believe,"
  red-arrow-and-circle thumbnails.

**The 50/50 model** (from the operator's own note, and correct):

- **~50% real material** — licensed/PD/CC photos, footage, documents, news, maps.
  Establishes that this is real.
- **~50% original built investigation** — reconstructions, timelines, evidence boards,
  relationship graphs, route maps, "what we know / don't know" panels, probability
  comparisons.

Real material anchors credibility; built material is the reason to watch *you*. It also
satisfies the "substantial original commentary / editing / educational value" test that
keeps a reused-footage channel monetised.

## §7 · The workflow — one spreadsheet, six tabs

One workbook (Excel or Sheets — structure > tool). Per-episode copies of the Source Log
and Asset Log also live in `episodes/<EP>/` next to the script, synced with the master.
Maps 1:1 onto the Runbook steps.

**TAB 1 · IDEA BANK** — every lead, scored & ranked (Runbook Step 0). Nothing gets made
that isn't top of the ranked list.
Columns: `ID · Date added · Pillar · Working title · Hook (1 sentence) · The unanswered
question · Novelty 1–5 · Evidence available 1–5 · Source-richness 1–5 · Best existing
version (channel + views) · Competition saturation 1–5 · Est. runtime · Priority score ·
Status`
Priority formula:
`= Novelty*2 + EvidenceAvailable*2 + SourceRichness*1.5 + (6 − CompetitionSaturation)*1.5`
sorted descending.
Status flow: `Raw → Triaged → Researching → Scripted → Shot-listed → Built → Published → Killed`

**TAB 2 · EPISODE PIPELINE** — the kanban, one row per greenlit episode.
Columns: `Ep code · Title · Stage · % complete · Current blocker · VO recorded? ·
Runtime planned/actual · Target publish · Actual publish · Notes`
Stage: `Intake → Script → VO → Reconcile → Build → Finish → Package → Scheduled → Live`
(= the Runbook gates; each "stops and waits" for sign-off).

**TAB 3 · SOURCE LOG** — research integrity, the spine. One row per factual claim in the
script; no row → doesn't ship.
Columns: `Ep · Claim (as stated in script) · Source (title, author, outlet) · URL ·
Archived URL · Source type · Date accessed · Confidence · Used in beat #`
Source type: `primary doc · court/inquest record · contemporaneous news · interview/
first-hand · secondary · forum lead (never a script source)`
Confidence: `Confirmed · Reported · Disputed · Rumour` — script wording must respect the
tag.

**TAB 4 · ASSET LOG** — every photo/clip/map/document/sound. Nothing placed in the edit
until its row reads `Cleared = Y`. Drives the description Sources block.
Columns: `Ep · Asset file · What it shows · Type · Source + collection · URL · Licence ·
Attribution string (exact) · Cost · Cleared? Y/N · Where used (beat + TC)`
Licence: `PD · CC0 · CC BY · CC BY-SA · licensed (paid) · fair-use claim · owned ·
AI-generated (disclosed)`

**TAB 5 · PACKAGING** (Runbook Step 5).
Columns: `Ep · Title A/B/C · vidIQ title score · Thumb concept A/B/C · Thumb text · Hook
(first 15s verbatim) · Description · Tags · Chapters · Pinned comment · Community post`
Target vidIQ title/thumb 90+; never fabricate a score. Description carries the full
**Sources & Media** block (also the monetisation evidence).

**TAB 6 · PERFORMANCE** — post-publish; feeds the next Idea Bank triage.
Columns: `Ep · Publish date · 24h/7d/30d views · CTR · Avg view duration · Avg % viewed ·
Subs gained · RPM · Revenue · Retention cliff (timestamp) · What we learned`

## §8 · Researching an episode

1. **Lead capture → Idea Bank.** Sources: `/last30days` on the two pillars;
   r/UnresolvedMysteries, r/RBI, r/UnsolvedMysteries; Wikipedia "List of unsolved…" /
   "List of people who disappeared…"; The Charley Project, NamUs; national news archives;
   contemporaneous newspaper databases; later, own comments. **A Reddit post is a lead,
   never a script** — it points at primary sources, it is not one.
2. **Triage (30–60 min/lead).** (a) Genuine unanswered question, or just atmosphere?
   (b) Does *primary* material exist — photos, docs, footage, transcripts, maps? (c) Who
   covered it, how big was the best version? Score. Greenlight only the top of the list.
3. **Deep research → Source Log.** Work outward from primary sources (official/inquest/
   court reports, contemporaneous news, archives, FOIA, first-hand). Wikipedia / other
   YT videos are *maps to sources*, never quoted as fact. Archive every source on capture
   (archive.today + Wayback). Tag confidence. **Log the contradictions — they are the
   episode.**
4. **The plagiarism line.** Paraphrase from primaries in your own words. Cite on screen
   and in the description. Never lift another creator's script or a book's phrasing. If
   one book/journalist did the original legwork, credit them by name in the video.
   Survival, not politeness.
5. **Script from the Source Log.** The `what we know / what we don't / what doesn't fit /
   leading explanations / the problem with each / the unresolved question` structure
   falls out of a good log. Then the Step 1 gate: `humanizer` → Binoculars → read aloud.
6. **Fact-check pass (before VO).** Re-walk the script line by line against the Source
   Log. Any unsourced sentence gets a source or gets cut. Then record VO.

**18-minute episode skeleton:** `0:00 cold open (the thing that doesn't make sense) ·
0:45 what happened · 2:30 place/people · 4:00 timeline · 6:00 first contradiction ·
8:00 evidence reconstruction · 10:30 leading explanation · 13:00 the problem with it ·
15:00 the alternative · 17:00 what remains unexplained`

## §9 · Footage & licensing — the sourcing ladder

Prefer the top rung; drop down only when you must; log every rung in the Asset Log.

| Rung | Source class | Where | Cost / obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Owned / code** | Remotion reconstructions, diagrams, maps, timelines, name redaction. The "50% original investigation." | Free · zero risk · maximise |
| 2 | **Public domain** | Library of Congress, National Archives (NARA), Prelinger Archives & Internet Archive Moving Image Archive, NASA, NOAA, Wikimedia Commons (PD-tagged), Flickr Commons. Pre-1930 US works; US federal works. | Free · no legal attribution, but log it |
| 3 | **Creative Commons** | Wikimedia Commons (check each file's exact tag), Openverse / CC Search, Flickr (CC filter), Vimeo (CC filter), YouTube (CC filter — verify uploader held rights). | Free + credit. CC BY / BY-SA need on-screen + description credit. **CC-NC = do not use** (monetised). |
| 4 | **Licensed archive (paid)** | AP Archive, Reuters, British Pathé, Getty/iStock, Pond5, Periscope Film, CriticalPast. | ~$50–150/clip, for the few shots only a real archive has. |
| 5 | **Fair use (news/factual, claimed deliberately)** | Short news clips you analyse. US 4-factor test; docs usually win even monetised *if* transformative, minimal length, factual source, no market substitution. | Legal defence, not a setting. Content ID still claims it; Fox has struck docs over 60-sec clips. Document 4-factor reasoning per clip; keep clips seconds long; expect to dispute. |
| 6 | **Stock (mood/texture only)** | Pexels, Pixabay, Videvo (free); Storyblocks, Artgrid (sub). Rain on a window, a forest, a night highway. | Free / sub · never as "evidence" |
| 7 | **AI-generated** | Non-evidentiary mood plates only. Disclosed via AI-use toggle + `ON_AI` block, SynthID intact. | Credibility landmine. Never anything a viewer could mistake for a real photo of a real person/place in the case. Sparingly, or not at all. |

**Attribution mechanics:**

- On screen: small per-shot corner credit — `Photo: LOC / [collection]`,
  `Footage: AP Archive (licensed)`. NIF's `SourceTag` component already does this.
- In the description: full **Sources & Media** block — every claim's source link + every
  CC asset's exact required attribution string + a licence link. Generated from Source
  Log + Asset Log by a script.
- That block = legal compliance + credibility signal + evidence of "original commentary +
  educational value" if the reused-content policy is ever applied.

**Two hard rules:**

- **Wikipedia text is CC BY-SA** — reuse/adapt its phrasing and you owe attribution *and*
  share-alike. Don't lift its sentences; write original prose from the primary sources it
  cites.
- **Never place anything you couldn't defend to a human reviewer.** Content ID
  over-matches even clean PD/CC content — dispute with Asset Log evidence, don't
  panic-delete; never build an episode that collapses if three claims land.

**What kills monetisation here:** graphic imagery, crime-scene photos, bodies, gore,
shock framing → per-video demonetisation (Jan 2026 rules; non-graphic dramatised
treatment of the same events stays fully eligible). You need none of it — Fascinating
Horror built 1.4M subs on "calm, factual, respectful, no disturbing imagery." House rule,
not a judgement call.

## §11 · First moves

**Open decisions — the operator's call:**

- **Channel slot.** Third channel, or replace M&M in the plan? Three live channels solo
  is a lot.
- **Name & launch pillars.** "The Unexplained, Investigated" umbrella, or tighter?
  Recommend launching on **Internet Mysteries + Real Unsolved/Disappearances**.
- **Face or faceless.** Recommend faceless + cloned VO (matches the stack; tier 1 is
  mostly faceless). The Danny Why reference is face-cam — skip that part.
- **Cadence.** Recommend **one episode every 3–4 weeks** to start.
- **Finish the sentence** the message ended on — *"…what is our —"*.

**30 / 60 / 90:**

1. **Days 0–30 · define & stock.** Pick the two pillars. Draft `identity.json` +
   `mystery-house-style` skill. Build the workbook. Fill the Idea Bank with 20 leads,
   triage, rank. Pick the pilot: rich PD/CC evidence + a real unanswered question (a
   historical or wilderness disappearance is the safest first outing — mostly PD
   material, low graphic-imagery risk).
2. **Days 30–60 · research & build the module.** Full research pass on the pilot (Source
   Log complete, every source archived). Build the `archival-media` module against the
   pilot's real shot list. Script → humanize → Binoculars → fact-check. Record VO.
3. **Days 60–90 · produce & ship the pilot.** Build in Remotion (NIF engine primitives +
   new module). Clear every Asset Log row before placing. Resolve finish.
   `claude-video-vision` QC. Package (vidIQ-scored title/thumb, full Sources description).
   Publish. Then Performance tab + go/no-go on the channel *from real numbers*: did the
   pipeline hold, did it clear monetisation clean, what did retention do.

**The one thing to get right:** the pilot is not trying to go viral. It tests whether the
research bar + licensing workload are carryable on top of a build that already takes
weeks. If it holds for one episode, the channel is real. If not, that's 90 days spent
instead of a year.
