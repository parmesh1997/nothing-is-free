## 1 · The channel in one line

> **Someone Always Pays: the greatest cons, heists and bubbles in history, and who
> really paid for them.**

Frauds, cons, heists and bubbles from American and British history, told as a case.
One case in three is a trick you still pay for today, told through its history (the
popcorn remake is one). **Channel description, first line:** *"History's greatest
cons, heists and bubbles, told as a case. Every episode asks one question: who really
paid?"*

## 2 · How we're unique, evergreen and monetisable

### 2.1 · The six pillars

Every channel in this lane asks **"how did he do it?"** We ask **"who really paid?"**
That one change sets the whole format:

| # | Pillar | What the viewer gets | Who else does it | Runbook |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The question** | We follow the money to the last person holding it. The obvious victim is suspect one, and cleared | Nobody. None of the 12 Poyais videos YouTube search returns (§8) asks who paid; most frame the con man ("most successful conman", "prince of lies") | §1.1, §4.2 |
| 2 | **Lucky is the mark** | Our character lives the con in its era, so you watch yourself get played | Theoretico's time-travelling skeleton proves the device (600k on *1 week in the Roman Empire*); nobody uses it for money crimes | §1.1, §4.2 |
| 3 | **The Ledger** | Every case ends on one ledger page: who paid, how much, then and in today's money | Nobody. It's our signature, like Oversimplified's ending | §2.8 |
| 4 | **The trick today** | The last minute shows the same mechanism where your money is now, which makes 200-year-old history personal | Some channels add a "today" coda, but none makes it the fixed ending | §4.2 |
| 5 | **Hand-built world, real documents** | Court records, bonds and newspapers on screen and in the description; nothing generated | The AI wave can't. FERN named *The Black Files* as an AI channel that copied it | §3 laws 3–4, 12 |
| 6 | **The challenge** | Before the reveal: "You know what we know. Who paid?" The pinned comment asks it again | Rare in history channels | §2.8, §14.1 |

**The moat is the combination.** Any one pillar can be copied. All six together are a
format, and a format with a recurring character builds a channel people come back to,
not just a video they find.

### 2.2 · Evergreen, by rule

- **Closed cases only**: nothing pending, no news peg (case test row 9).
- **Titles without dates or years of upload.** The con's name, which people search
  every month, goes in the description: *gregor macgregor* gets 14k searches a month,
  *charles ponzi* 26k, *south sea bubble* 9.7k.
- **The trick today is a pattern, never a named company or a current event**, so it
  doesn't date and doesn't defame.
- **A *Case files* playlist**, in order, as the binge path (§14.1).

### 2.3 · Monetisable, by rule

| Risk | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Inauthentic / repetitive content** (YouTube 2025–26) | Every case has its own scene of the crime (§2.4), the choices are human-directed (§3 law 12), and the effort is visible (law 13). No template episodes |
| **The AI label** | YouTube is starting to detect synthetic voices (FERN; ElevenLabs uses SynthID). Tick the altered-content label on every upload (§13.3). **Better: record the narration yourself.** A human voice is the strongest sign that a person made the video |
| **Advertiser-friendly** | Violence off screen; deaths described, never shown; nothing violent in the thumbnail or title; the dark act uses the ink-wash treatment (§4.9.1) |
| **Defamation** | A crime is stated only where a court or the historical record established it; no living private person is accused (row 6); the disclaimer (§13.3) |
| **RPM** | Upload as **Education** (about $6 per 1,000 US views in vidIQ's model, against $2.50 for Entertainment). Money-crime titles attract finance advertisers (Business, about $8) |
| **Paid promotion** | None (§1.4). Your 76 ad views watched 11.6%, half the organic rate |

**The arithmetic of the Partner Programme** (1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch
hours in 12 months): at 13 minutes and about 30% watched, one view is about 4 minutes,
so 4,000 hours is about 60,000 views. **Eight cases at 7,500 views each, or one at
60,000.** The research says a single case in this lane can do that (§3).

## 10 · How we judge it

The 7-day gates: **CTR ≥ 6%** and **share watched ≥ 45%**. Each case is compared with the
others at the same age.

| After three cases | It means | Change one thing (§14.3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CTR good, retention low | The case doesn't hold | The script: read the drops with `dip-map.mjs` |
| CTR low, retention good | The packaging undersells it | Title and thumbnail: the feed test, then *Test & compare* after ~1,000 impressions |
| Both low | The idea | Re-weight the case score against the results |
| Both good, impressions small | The test is still small | Nothing. Keep shipping |
| **Suggested traffic still near zero** | YouTube hasn't found who to recommend us to | Check the six pillars are on screen in every case; tighten one viewer, one format |

## 12 · Channel metadata and SEO (paste-ready)

### 12.4 · Every episode: the SEO template

1. **Title**, 60 characters or fewer. It carries a hook, the payer or what they paid
   for, and the case's searched name where it reads naturally (runbook §6.0). No year
   of upload. Draft three and score them in vidIQ.
2. **Description:**

```
[Line 1: the hook with the primary keyword, e.g. "In 1822, Gregor MacGregor sold London a country that didn't exist."]
[Line 2: the question, e.g. "250 settlers sailed for Poyais. Who really paid for it?"]

Every Sunday: one case from history's greatest cons, heists and bubbles, and who really paid.
▶ Case files (every case): [playlist link]

CHAPTERS
0:00 The crime
[…the case-file tabs, from 03_transcript/chapters.md]

SOURCES
[Author, title, year] for every figure, with years

CREDITS
Music: [title, artist, licence] · Archival: [credit lines]

ABOUT THIS VIDEO
Narration is a synthetic voice; every picture is made by hand, nothing generated.
[The disclaimer from runbook §13.3]

#history #truecrime #scam
```

3. **Tags:** 10–15, under 500 characters. The primary keyword and its variants, the
   con's name, the era, two channel-core terms, and the channel name.
4. **Hashtags:** three at most. They show above the title: `#history #truecrime #scam`
   (swap the third for `#heist` or `#bubble` by case type).
5. **Captions:** upload the corrected whisper SRT. Accurate captions are words YouTube
   can index; the auto-captions mangle names like "Poyais".
6. **File name:** name the upload after the primary keyword (`gregor-macgregor-poyais.mp4`).
   It's a small signal, but a free one.
7. **Chapters:** the case-file tab names. Chapter titles are indexed, so put the case's
   words in them (*"The prince of Poyais"*, not *"Part 2"*).
8. **Cards:** one card at the red herring cleared, pointing to the previous case.
   **End screen:** the previous case plus *Case files* (§14.1).
9. **Pinned comment:** *"Who did you suspect before the reveal?"*
10. **Thumbnail:** one frame of one object from our own scene, at most two words,
    painted or toon-lit (§14.1). One at publish; test after about 1,000 impressions.
