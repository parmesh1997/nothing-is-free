# STEP 3 — SCRIPT

## 3.1 — Pattern (rotate, never twice running)

**Pattern 1 — THE DESCENT** *(Model 1)*
> Cold open: a comparison making a normal business look impossible.
> `[Industry norm] + [This one doesn't do that] + [So what is it actually]`
> Body: descend one rung at a time. Each rung cheaper and faster than the one above, each with its own number and **its own price unit** — per item, then per pound, then per ton. The changing unit *is* the story.
> Counter-argument near the bottom. Close on the reversal.
> **Best for:** supply chains, resale, waste, logistics, physical ladders.

**Pattern 2 — THE LEDGER** *(Model 2)*
> Cold open: the pitch as believers state it, taken seriously — then the strange part.
> `[The dream, stated fairly] + [But most people who try it…] + [The number they never ran]`
> Body: promise the deliverables explicitly. Run arithmetic until a number lands badly. Name real operators at two scales. Run the failure pattern. Two complete scenarios.
> Close: what it actually costs. Not "don't," not "do."
> **Best for:** business models a viewer might enter, unit economics.

**Pattern 3 — THE TRACE** *(systems)*
> Cold open: one transaction the viewer has made, priced exactly.
> `[Your transaction] + [The hands it passes through] + [What's left]`
> Body: follow the money in order, running total visible. The invisible party appears around 60%.
> Close: the total skim, and who set it.
> ⭐ **Strongest moat.** Most on-thesis.

## 3.2 — Beat sheet

| Beat | Timing | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** | 0:00–0:20 | Counter-intuitive claim, flat, real number inside 15s. **Never a greeting, never "in this video."** Visually 100% BUILT, no Lucky |
| The question | 0:20–0:45 | The open loop, plainly. **Lucky enters here, on the drop** |
| Beats 1–2 | — | Each owns a number the previous didn't |
| **Mid-tease** | ~40% | "There's a third party taking a cut, and it isn't who you'd guess" |
| **Engagement beat** | ~45–50% | Prediction question, 3.3 |
| Beats 3–4 | — | Escalating. In Pattern 1 the unit changes here |
| **Counter-argument** | ~70% | Refuse the easy villain |
| **The reversal** | ~85% | The Step 2.6 line. **Music stops. Screen goes to deep ink. Lucky is on screen** |
| The implication | ~60s | "Now you'll see it every time you…" |
| **The bridge** | final 30s | One line into the named next episode |
| CTA | final 15s | Names previous episode. Number per Appendix F |

## 3.3 — Engagement beat

Model 1: *"What is the single best thing you've ever pulled out of a thrift store, and what did you pay for it? I want the item and the price."*

Copy the mechanism, not the wording: **a prediction or confession tied to the viewer's own transaction**, answerable in six words. "Which industry next?" is the weakest possible version.

## 3.4 — ⭐ Write for Lucky, and assign scene weight

As you write, mark the moments where **Lucky is physically in the scene** — at least one per beat. She's the one dropping the bags, paying the $9, tapping the card, standing in the queue, opening the bill.

Marker format:

```
[SCENE: Lucky, wide, at the donation door, four bags | weight: medium]
```

**Scene weight is assigned here, not at Step 6.** Light / Medium / Heavy, per §2 of the editing workflow.

| Weight | Layers | Target share |
|---|---|---|
| **Light** | 2–3 — base + Lucky, or base + type | ~35% |
| **Medium** | 4 — base + plate + Lucky + icon | ~45% |
| **Heavy** | 5–6 — full stack, or chart at full frame | ~20% |

The full six-layer stack on every scene is what produces viewer fatigue, and it reads as assembled rather than directed because nothing is ever emphasised. Rotating weight is what makes the heavy scenes land.

If a beat has no place for Lucky it becomes a chart beat, and a script that is all chart beats is the anti-model's shape. **Every "you've done this" line should have her doing it.**

## 3.5 — Voice rules

Second person where the viewer is the customer. Contractions. Short sentences. Speakable aloud.

**Name the number, never "a lot."** **One source class per beat** — "in its 2024 annual report," "according to the trade body's own survey." **Let the bad number land, then stop talking** — Model 2's best moment has no rescue after it. Silence after a number is the cheapest retention device you have. **USD default.**

### Hard bans

| Ban | Why |
|---|---|
| Any advice — "you should," "buy," "avoid," "invest in" | Fastest demonetisation route in finance |
| Price targets, tickers as recommendations, return projections | Same |
| "They don't want you to know" | Advertiser-hostile *and* the slop signature |
| Unsourced accusation of a named person or company | Defamation |
| Round-number invention — "about a billion," "roughly 40%" with no source | Fabricated-study equivalent |
| **Concept-listing** — "Business model number one… number two…" | The anti-model |
| "In today's fast-paced world," "delve," "moreover," "it's important to note" | AI-tell |
| "But here's where it gets interesting," "and that's the real business" | Finance-specific convergence phrases. Grep for them |
| Greeting, "in this video," "let's dive in" | Kills the cold open |

## 3.6 — ⭐ The reversal law

**Every episode ends on a reversal.** Not a summary. The viewer's assumed role in the transaction flips.

| Weak close | Reversal |
|---|---|
| "So that's how thrift stores make money." | "You never were the customer. You were the supplier." |
| "That's how free apps monetise." | "The app isn't free. The person paying just isn't you." |
| "And that's the economics of TV." | "You always paid for TV. It was in your cable bill whether you switched it on or not." |

A summary is templatable. A reversal requires having found something. **This is the hardest element for a content mill to fake and it is why the channel exists.**

**Delivered with:** word count + runtime @150wpm · density count · loop table · wallet-recognition count · `[SCENE:]` markers listed with weights · scene weight distribution · mid-tease and engagement beat marked · figures tagged to Step 2 rows · reversal and bridge stated separately.

# STEP 5 — VOICEOVER

**Record before the shot list.** Real VO gives real timing.

1. One pass per beat.
2. **Numbers get their own take.** Most-rewound, most-flubbed.
3. **Rehearse the reversal.** The only line that has to be delivered, not read.
4. Output is a **timed transcript** — the input to Step 6.

Fairlight chain (saved preset): Voice Isolation 30–50% · high-pass 80Hz · −3dB @300Hz if muddy · +2dB shelf above 8kHz · compressor 3:1 catching 4–6dB · dialogue to −16 LUFS · one flattened WAV.

⚠️ Cloned voice = synthetic audio. Disclosure ON, Step 12.

# STEP 6.5 — ASSET TRIAGE ⛔ HARD GATE

Every shot is sorted into exactly one lane **before** anything is generated.

| Lane | Signal | Action | Expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BUILT** | A number, a system, a comparison, typography | React component | ~30% |
| **STAGED** | A person, a place or an object, static | Generate a still, animate in Resolve | ~50% |
| **DOCUMENT** | The evidence exists as a public document | Screenshot and annotate. **Always preferred over illustrating a document** | ~10% |
| **MOTION** | The idea genuinely requires movement | Flow clip, 6–8s | ~8% |
| **REWRITE** | Abstract because you *wrote* it abstract | Rewrite the line to a concrete image | ~2% |

**REWRITE is checked first, always.** Free, improves the writing, removes the shot.

**MOTION is checked last and grudgingly.** Would a still with a slow push and an icon carry this? Almost always yes. Every clip you don't generate is style drift you don't risk.

**FAIL conditions**
- MOTION above 12 clips
- Any shot sent to generation without a lane
- Any lane assignment putting a generated asset on a shot carrying a spoken figure
- Any shot in 0:00–0:30 assigned to anything but BUILT

# STEP 7 — ASSET GENERATION

## 7.1 — Order, never varied
**Library → React → Document → Generate stills → Generate motion.**

Generation is last because everything before it is free, faster and more controllable.

## 7.2 — Library first
Run the shot list against the asset index.

**Reuse limits ⛔** — no asset reused within 3 episodes · no more than 15% of an episode's assets reused · icons exempt.

## 7.3 — React batch
Render this episode's charts and **all Figure Cards in one pass** from the Number Log JSON.

```bash
npx remotion render charts.json ./out/ep07 --codec=prores --prores-profile=4444
```

## 7.4 — Document capture
Filings, published tables, regulator data. Screenshot at source resolution, annotate in Resolve. Attribute on screen in Mono.

## 7.5 — ⭐ Still generation, targeted to the compositing tier

Prompt grammar is mandatory: **global block + subject + negative block**, plus the fixed character sheet verbatim on every Lucky shot, plus the canonical reference set. `Visual_System_and_Craft_v2.md` §5.

**Generation is targeted to the compositing tier** (`Editing_and_Animation_Workflow.md` §3):

| Tier | Instruction |
|---|---|
| **0** | Request a **transparent PNG** for every icon, object and prop |
| **1** | Put the episode's **base hex** in the prompt for anything sitting on that base. Composites with no key |
| **2** | Flat **`#FF00FF` magenta** for anything that must move over a different base. **Never green** — it spills into warm skin and cream, and the whole palette is warm |
| **3** | Magic Mask 2 + Render in Place, for complex shots only |

**Curation, per asset:**
- Hands and fingers correct
- **Zero text, signage, numbers or currency anywhere in frame**
- The orange garment present and the right orange
- Palette inside the five
- Painterly, not drifting toward photoreal or 3D
- Doesn't resemble a real identifiable person

Discard freely. Generation is cheap; a bad asset on screen for six seconds is not.

## 7.6 — Motion generation (8–12 clips)

# APPENDIX I — SCHEDULE & THE DECEMBER DEADLINE

## The real deadline is 20 December 2026

From 1 February 2027, YPP entry becomes 1,000 subs + **8,000** qualified watch hours (or 20M Shorts views). Existing partners are unaffected; 31 January is *their* terms-acceptance deadline, not yours.

You must be **accepted**, not merely applied, before 1 February. Review typically takes about a month. **Submit by ~20 December.**

Safety net: the 500-subscriber fan-funding tier (3,000 watch hours) is unchanged.

## Cadence

| Episodes | Cadence |
|---|---|
| 1–8 | **1 long-form/week.** Two a week collapses quality, quality produces breakouts, breakouts produce watch hours |
| 9+ | Reassess. Only go to 2/week if the box holds *and* a breakout has landed |

Never faster than 1 per 5 days. Never the same day as psychology.

## Slots

| | Day | ET | IST | What |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | **Thursday** | **4:00 PM** | Fri 1:30 AM | Long-form |
| Secondary | Monday | 12:00 PM | Mon 9:30 PM | 1–2 Shorts |

Thursday 4 PM ET is 1 PM Pacific and 9 PM UK, and gives the video Thursday night, Friday and the weekend while the impression test runs.

Schedule it and sleep. At 7 AM IST Friday it's 9:30 PM Thursday US East — five hours old, audience awake. That's your comment window. Holds after US DST ends 1 November.

**Hold the slot unchanged for six uploads.** After six, read first-48-hour impressions and CTR — not views.

## Pace table

4,000 hours ≈ 240,000 watch minutes. At ~5.5 min average view duration, **~43,000 views by 20 December.**

| By end of | Views | Hours | Subs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month 1 (4 eps) | 2,000 | 180 | 60 |
| Month 2 (8 eps) | 6,000 | 550 | 180 |
| Month 3 (13 eps) | 16,000 | 1,450 | 450 |
| Month 4 (17 eps) | **43,000** | **4,000** | **1,000** |

Not linear, and not supposed to be. It assumes **two or three videos break out to 20K+**. Grinding can't produce that; packaging and reversals can. Below 8,000 cumulative views at month 3 means thumbnails, not effort.

Long-form converts ~650 subs at typical rates. **Shorts carry the subscriber half.**

## Paid promotion — hold until episode 8

Evidence conflicts on whether promoted watch time counts. The more specific reading is that ad views count as public views but **watch time from ad campaigns does not count toward the 4,000 valid public watch hours** — only organic long-form does. Practitioner reports add that promotion drags average view duration down on the promoted video and on whatever the ad-clicker watches next.

Bad asymmetry against your deadline. **Spend it on thumbnail iteration.** If you promote: after episode 8, best flagship, US-only, treated as audience seeding.

## Decision points

- **After episode 6:** impressions and CTR. Under 3% CTR with healthy impressions = thumbnails.
- **After episode 8:** cadence review.
- **After episode 10:** 30–40 min unlocks if a flagship held retention above 40%.
- **Episodes 15–20:** if one pillar returns 2–3× subscriber count, niche into it.
- **20 December:** apply regardless of where the numbers sit.
