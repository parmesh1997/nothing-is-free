## 4. Description (§13.5)

```
Google Maps has never charged you a cent — no subscription, no paywall, it has
never once asked for a card number. And analysts estimate it makes around
eleven billion dollars a year. So this episode answers one question: if the map
never charges you, who is it charging? The answer is three separate meters
running every time it is open — one on the apps, one on the businesses, and one
that runs on you — and a role you were never told you had.

WHAT THIS EPISODE ANSWERS
• Why almost every app, car and "find us" box runs on Google's map, and what
  each map load costs them
• What changed on 16 July 2018, when the free daily allowance ended and a card
  on file became mandatory
• What Uber disclosed to its investors — including $58M paid to Google over
  three years — and what it spent trying to escape
• Why Amazon, Meta, Microsoft and TomTom stopped competing on maps and pooled
  their data instead (the Overture Maps Foundation)
• How the pin on top of your search results gets there, and what "Sponsored"
  actually costs the advertiser
• What your location trail is worth, why turning off Location History didn't
  stop the tracking, and the $391.5M + $93M settlements that followed
• Why 2 billion people a month can't simply switch to another map

Previous episode: Your "Free" TV Pays Four Companies Before Your Show Even Starts
```
